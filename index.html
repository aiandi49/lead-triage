      Before you send

      How to send these without breaking Craigslist's rules
      • Send from the post's own reply button, or the contact method the post gives. Text only if the post invites texts.
      • One message per post. Follow up only if they answer.
      • Tie the message to what the post says. The drafts say plainly that you are offering a tool, not applying for the job.
      • Craigslist's terms (last updated August 16, 2019; I read the live page on September 20, 2026) ban unsolicited communications. They set $25 for each unsolicited email sent to an address obtained from Craigslist, and $500 for each unsolicited text or call made using Craigslist user information.
      • I cannot tell you how Craigslist would treat any single message, and I am not a lawyer. Marketing texts to cell phones may also fall under separate consumer-protection rules, which I have not researched.
      What I set aside, and why
      • Focus groups, surveys, research studies. Roughly a quarter of the gig titles. People selling their time, not businesses buying tools.
      • Casting, models, actors, performers. Roughly a fifth of the gig titles.
      • Commission-only and "earn $X a week" pitches. Includes the repeated "Entry Level Trading Analyst" posts. These recruit sellers; they do not buy.
      • Donor and surrogacy recruiting. Not a fit and not something to pitch.
      • National firms and staffing agencies. Valet Living, Safeguard Properties, Solid Personnel, Southwest Contractor Staffing. A Craigslist reply is unlikely to reach a buyer.
      • Lead-generation and platform recruiters. Lawn-care "pros" posts, the 813-number plumber and electrician post, the Rentatech handyman posts. They look like platforms, though I did not verify that.
      • Homeowner requests. Titles like "Looking for Roofer 85260" and "Fix leaky hose bib" read as customers, not trade businesses.
      • Bid invitations. "Invitation to bid, HOA perimeter" and "Seeking bids for sanding" are the buyer side. The contractors who answer are your audience, but you cannot reach them from here.
      • Content, writing, and social media gigs. Left out on purpose, except remote assistant roles where a small tool could take over routine work.
      • Recruiting platforms and national brands. Wayfair Home Services "LLC owners" ads, LeafHome canvassers, Allied Universal, Sevita, and the trucking and driver posts. Big employers or platforms, not small businesses you can reach.
      • Caregiving, restaurants, salons, retail. Almost all in person, so they cannot be solved remotely.
